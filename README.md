<h2 align="center">About</h2>

I am a **Counselling Psychologist** and MSc Psychology candidate (result awaited) specialising in **Clinical & Counselling Psychology** at West Bengal State University. My practice is grounded in careful assessment, ethical case formulation and evidence-based intervention, built through over a year of supervised clinical experience across hospital, university, school and online settings.

I work across the full clinical workflow: intake and case history, Mental Status Examination, psychometric and projective assessment, DSM-5-guided formulation under supervision, and medico-legal and disability documentation. Alongside practice, I conduct field-based quantitative and qualitative research on culture, stigma and help-seeking in underserved Indian communities.

My long-term aim is to make mental health services accessible beyond the city, beginning with rural outreach and, eventually, a community mental health facility.

<h2 align="center">Featured Research</h2>

<details>
<summary><b>The Uncertainty Trap: Why Distorted Minds Turn to the Supernatural</b> &nbsp;·&nbsp; 2026</summary>

<br/>

A field study of why people attribute unexplained illness or behaviour to spirits or curses rather than to medical causes.

| | |
|:--|:--|
| **Focus** | Supernatural attribution of illness and behaviour |
| **Method** | Large-scale field study |
| **Scope** | Rural, sub-urban and urban Bengal |
| **Key Insight** | Intolerance of uncertainty, not distorted thinking, emerged as the driver of this belief |
| **Impact** | Points toward a new, low-cost approach to mental health outreach in underserved Indian communities |

The study reframes supernatural explanations as a response to the discomfort of not knowing, which has direct implications for how outreach and psychoeducation are designed for communities with limited access to care.

</details>
